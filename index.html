Global Energy Transition and Critical Material Demand

KIT · Institute for Technology Assessment and Systems Analysis (ITAS)
Scientific Assistant · October 2026–present

I compare global 100% renewable and net-zero energy scenarios toward 2050–2100, examining technology deployment, energy storage requirements and the associated demand for critical raw materials.

Explore the research

Research question

How do global energy-transition pathways translate into future technology deployment, material demand and potential supply risks?

My contribution

  • Review international studies and extract quantitative projections for solar PV, wind, hydropower, Power-to-X, electricity generation and storage.
  • Build comparative datasets documenting installed capacities, generation, storage requirements and scenario assumptions.
  • Compare technology pathways and deployment assumptions across 100% renewable and net-zero energy studies.
  • Assess critical-material requirements associated with large-scale renewable-energy deployment and future technology demand.
  • Link energy-transition scenarios with raw-material demand and potential supply risks for strategic energy technologies.

Research objective

Connect long-term energy-transition scenarios with their material requirements to support assessment of future technology demand and resource constraints.
